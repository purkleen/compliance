# Regulatory Risk Assessment

A Typeform-style form for the "Upcoming Legal and Systemic Changes" part of the risk management matrix.

Open `index.html` in a browser. It is "Choose your way", a start page that explains why people are asked ("Why we are asking you") and then offers three tiles that lead to the three versions of the form: `questions-first.html` (Questions first), `version-03.html` (Choose risks first) and `free-hand.html` (Free hand). A menu next to the title switches between all four, and each form has a small "Restart" button in the bottom-left corner and a "Save draft" button in the top bar, which keeps what has been entered in that browser. Older addresses still work: `choose.html` redirects to the front page and `version-02.html` to Free hand. Two earlier versions, `alternative-01.html` and `original.html`, are kept but no longer linked.

- **Form (Free hand, `free-hand.html`):** after an intro, the start screen offers a risk search (also matching synonyms), "Add new risk" with the risks of the person's own area shown first in a highlighted "Your area" panel (set by the link, for example `free-hand.html#cat-main`; without a link the first area is used), "Browse all risk options", and two shortcuts: upload documents or leave an audio recording. Picking a risk opens a "Risk description" screen with one text box, attachments and an optional follow-up question from the assistant. Each added risk appears in a list at the bottom of the start screen, with "Submit risks" under it.
- **Form (Choose risks first, `version-03.html`):** opens on an intro that explains how the process goes, then goes step by step: choose risks from a multi-select list ("Add a risk" names one that is not on the list; the person's own area first, set by the link, for example `version-03.html#cat-main`; search also matches synonyms and can add an own risk), describe any other risks in free text, review the selection, add documents or an audio recording, then describe each selected risk on its own screen, where the assistant may ask a follow-up question. A summary table at the end can be edited before submitting, which leads to a dashboard of submitted forms with PDF download.
- **Form (questions first):** `questions-first.html` starts with five questionnaire-style questions, one per screen, counted as "Question: 1/5" (a rating, yes/no and multiple choice, usable from the keyboard). Once an answer is picked, an optional box for details and "Add supporting documents" appear; Back and Next question sit at the right of one shared column. The answers are matched to risks: detected risks are put on the person's list and others are offered as suggestions. The person can then describe other risks in free text, add documents or an audio recording, and describe each risk on its own screen, where the assistant may ask a follow-up question. A summary of the answers and the risks closes the flow.
- **View switch and side menu:** a switch in the top bar changes between the Employee view (only the form) and the Compliance team view. The prototype opens in the Compliance team view. Switching to Employee opens "Choose your way", where the person picks a form; a person's own link or an area link opens the form directly. The menu for switching between versions is hidden. The Compliance team view has a collapsible menu on the left: Dashboard; Forms (Review submissions, with a count of submissions waiting for review, Track forms and Templates); External sources (Legislation watch); Matrix (Risk matrix, Matrix version history and AI guidance); Labelling (Tagging and Labeling, both marked "Coming soon" and not open yet, and Synonyms); in that view the top bar hides the title and the version menu; it collapses to icons and opens over the page on small screens. The switch is a prototype convenience, not access control.
- **Dashboard:** for the compliance team. Tiles summarise forms sent, opened, saved, submitted and waiting for review. Below them are a "New submissions" card (the three latest submissions, with a dot on ones not opened yet and a link to each) and a "Latest matrix version" card (who published it and when, a written summary of the changes with "Read more", a "New version" marker until the matrix is opened, and a link to the Risk matrix), then a "Legislation changes" card (a summary and the last three tracked changes) and an "Industry news" card (a summary of articles found on pharma industry pages and news sites; sample articles in this prototype). The Track forms page shows each person's status (sent, opened, draft saved, submitted) as a pill, with filters by department and stage, sortable columns, 10 rows per page and a three-dot menu per row (email, copy link, remind, remove). The "Waiting for review" tile opens the pending submissions. The "Send a form" button opens a separate page: by default each person gets the template prepared for their department ("Corresponding template"), or one area can be chosen for everyone; then pick people (the list can be searched, filtered by department and selected all at once) or type email addresses, which become removable tags, and add a message. Forms sent this way use "Choose your way". One link is recorded per person. In this prototype tracking lives in the browser and email is handed to the visitor's email app.
- **Legislation watch:** a Notifications card with a count on the menu item, and below it a separate card with the addresses to watch, with add and remove. "Check now" is the main button at the top. In this prototype the pages are not read; alerts are sample data.
- **Review submissions:** a split screen with the submitted forms waiting for review on the left (approved ones leave the list; when none are left, a centred "No new submissions." message offers "Send a form"; in Polish the screen is called "Nowe zgłoszenia") (name and department, date and number of risks, with a "New" marker until the form is first opened in this browser) and on the right a summary that lists the risks with cited sources (longer lists show five with "See all"). "Approve" and "Edit answers" sit next to the person's name; a three-dot menu holds the rest. Reviewers can reject a submission with a reason, email extra questions (the page opens the email app with the message ready), resend the form to a person, jump to tagging, and open the version from before any edit in the history. The Synonyms page sets the words the form's search matches for each risk, shown as removable pills; inside Claude it can suggest more with AI. The Templates page gives a form link set to each area.
- **Risk matrix:** the company risk matrix (areas, risk categories and risks with all assessment columns, filled with example data in Polish), with search, an area filter, the column guidance from the template and zoom. Area and Risk category stay in place when the table scrolls sideways; a pin in each of their headers freezes or unfreezes that column. Clicking a cell opens a side panel with the employee submissions behind its content (why each one supports it, with a quote and a link to the submission and back), and links to the legal act's official page as a link pill where one is known. The cell can be edited, and while editing any number of website links and documents can be added to it or removed (they show as pills under the value; in this prototype documents keep only their name and can be opened until the page is reloaded); the score and level are recalculated from Impact and Probability, and each cell has a history of changes. Clicking a change in "History of changes" centres its cell in the matrix, with a button to go back to the list. Edits stay unpublished (marked with a dot and a light blue tint) until "Publish" opens a side panel listing the changes, takes an optional note and saves them as the next version.
- **Matrix version history:** every publish is a new version. The list of versions on the left shows who made each one, when, and what changed; the right side shows the whole matrix as it was at the chosen version, with the cells changed in that version highlighted and an option to show only the changed risks; opening a version scrolls the matrix to the nearest change. "Summary" lists every change in a version, grouped by risk, with the text before and after; "Show in matrix" centres that cell in the version shown and keeps the summary open. Clicking a highlighted cell shows just that cell's change. "Retrieve this version" brings an earlier version back as a new version, so nothing is lost. Four earlier versions by other team members are example data.
- **AI guidance:** the risk matrix again, where clicking a column name, a risk name or a cell opens notes that tell the AI where information from the forms belongs: what belongs there and what does not, an example, tags and labels, and documents and links. "What the AI sees" shows the text the AI would be given for that place, and a purple dot marks places with guidance. Nothing in the prototype places form content into the matrix with AI yet; the guidance is stored and previewed.
- **Tagging:** tag each answer against the taxonomy and manage the tags. The first three tag groups are the matrix columns, so tags place an answer in the matrix.
- **Labeling:** a Label Studio-style screen for marking phrases in an answer with labels, with hotkeys, a regions panel and Submit / Skip per task.
- A dark mode toggle sits at the top right. Icons are from Lucide.

## Demo limits

- Follow-up questions are scripted here. Live AI follow-ups and summaries, and saving to a shared store, only work when the page runs as a Claude artifact.
- Outside Claude, submitted forms, sent forms, tags and labels are kept in the browser's local storage, so each browser sees only its own.
- Attached files are not uploaded anywhere; only their names are recorded.
- Every text box in the employee forms has a microphone for dictation. It uses the browser's speech recognition (Chrome, Edge, Safari) and is switched off inside Claude, where the microphone is not available.
- Matrix edits, versions and AI guidance are kept in the browser's local storage.

## Polish version

Every page has an EN / PL switch in the top bar. The pages are built in English and a translation layer (a dictionary of about 900 texts plus rules for counts and Polish plurals, embedded at the top of each page) swaps the text for Polish as it appears; dates use Polish format, search also matches Polish risk names, and inside Claude the assistant is asked to answer in Polish. The choice is kept in the browser; `?lang=pl` or `?lang=en` in the address sets it. Text people type, and the search synonyms, stay as they are.
